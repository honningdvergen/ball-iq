The five-star verdict

No — not today, and I can name what stops it in four sentences rather than four hundred. First, the honest good news, because it is unusual and it is yours: I verified by two independent routes that there are no ads, no paywall, no sign-up wall to play, no permission prompt in the first minute, and zero JS errors across a dozen full first-runs. That is four of the six things that reliably cost quiz apps a star, already clean, and most competitors fail at least two of them. Your ceiling is high. Your floor is what is being set right now. What stops the fifth star, in order of how loudly a reviewer would say it: 1. The store build has a dead end. A new user signing in with Apple or Google on App Store 1.6.0 or Play vc20 meets a full-screen, non-dismissible step that pre-fills their real name from their Apple ID — space and all — and then refuses it for containing a space. No skip, no escape, and the flag survives a reload. That is a one-star review that writes itself: "couldn't get past the sign-up screen." The fix exists in the repo and reaches nobody. This is also the single most plausible explanation for 103 accounts that never played a game, though nobody instrumented that screen so I cannot prove causation. 2. The bank contradicts itself, and players are telling us. 67.5 reports per 1,000 sessions and rising — roughly one complaint every fifteen sessions from 92 active players. More damning: over half of the reports that carry a pick come from players who answered CORRECTLY. They are not confused, they are correcting us. Two independent detectors converged on ~36 questions that hand you the answer inside the question ("In which French city is Olympique de Marseille based?"), 5 stems containing their own answer verbatim, and 15 club-pack questions whose answer is the club you just picked. This is your own sentence — "not insult the intellect of our users" — and it is currently the most measurable thing wrong with the app. 3. The emotional peak of the product looks like a form error. I confirmed this in the stylesheet: somebody designed a proper tinted feedback tray for the wrong-answer moment at app.css:658, and two !important declarations two lines above it switch it off. So the verdict of every single question — the moment that decides whether a miss feels like learning or like being marked wrong — renders as a naked 12.5px orphan line. On the same screen, the most-tapped control in the whole app (.opt) has no press state at all, while 46 other elements in the same stylesheet depress under a thumb. Nobody writes "missing :active state" in a review. They write "feels a bit basic" and give four stars. 4. The hero feature runs dry in two rounds. Man United's eligible pool is 24 questions against a 10-question round. 81 of 86 club packs give under four fresh rounds, and then the app silently repeats without saying so — while the web club page, doing the same thing, ends honestly with "That is every Man United question we have here." The surface being rated is the one that pretends. A football fan who used Ball IQ for a week today would give it four stars and mean it kindly. They would say: genuinely good questions, lovely daily habit, feels a bit rough, and I hit a couple of questions that were wrong or gave themselves away. That is a fair review of what we have. Everything above is fixable in about two weeks of the kind of work you already do well.

  8. Look at the Home header and the Profile screen and just judge them. Header gear, streak flame, avatar, the emoji density, the '46' rating painted in the muted grey token. These are the frames that appear in App Store screenshots and in the first sixty seconds. Only you can say whether they look like a product you would pay attention to.

Stop doing

  • Stop fixing something in one half and calling it done. This is now six documented instances and it is the single most expensive habit in the project: the Footle copy (fixed in five places, missed the sixth — and the marketing surface is STILL wrong in seven more, verified live on balliq.app right now); the username space rule (fixed in the modal, not in Profile, until this morning); the keyboard insets across three modes; the colour-only feedback glyphs; the streak label (fixed between two surfaces, third one shipped); the settings-defaults merge (three separate reviewers found the same App.jsx:9530 line independently and none noticed the other two). The rule that actually works is the one you used this morning: when a fix touches an invariant, write the test against the CLASS, not the instance, and grep the whole tree — including src/marketing/ — before declaring the count. A one-line test that cannot rot beats a careful memory every time.
  • Stop counting web deploys as delivery. Roughly 40 commits landed and the store builds are unchanged, so the honest answer to "what did this week do for our 500 downloads" is nothing. Your own memory file already says this and the panel did it anyway, fourteen times over. From now on, every finding gets one of three labels before it gets a fix: web-now, native-at-upload, or nobody-yet. If a sprint's labels are all "native-at-upload" and no upload is planned, that sprint has not shipped.
  • Stop trusting a detector's first run, and stop trusting a zero. Ten instances now. In this round alone: the leak audit reported 0 unguarded packs while the Home-featured Summer 2026 pack leaked in 38.4% of sessions; one reviewer's giveaway detector ran at 24% precision on its first pass and its first self-test printed "detector is broken" because the seeded examples were not actually giveaways; another reviewer nearly headlined a consent-banner bug that was purely an artifact of requestAnimationFrame never firing in a hidden browser pane. The reviewers who caught their own errors all did the same thing: ran a control in the same call. Make that non-optional — every negative result needs a deliberately broken input proving the check can fire.
  • Stop re-auditing what has already been audited. SEO ranking fundamentals are clean and have been re-confirmed three times; the localisation thesis is settled; the MP capacity question is resolved. Every hour spent re-confirming a known-good area is an hour not spent on the 47% of accounts that never finish a game. The bank, the first session and the native build are where the remaining points are.
  • Stop shipping a new mode without the feel checklist. The evidence is unambiguous: haptic and sound density is 48/19 inside App.jsx and 2/0, 4/0, 7/0, 1/0, 0/0 in everything built afterwards — Trail, Mystery, Stadiums, the nav, Login. The polish layer exists and is good; it just stops at the boundary of the file it was written in. Four lines in the definition of done for any new mode — press state, haptic, sound, reveal timing — and the pattern stops repeating at mode seven. Same for the icon layer: 674 emoji against 28 SVGs is not a style choice, it is what happens when nobody owns it.

The biggest risk

That you upload 1.7.0 to fix the sign-up dead end and ship a fresh native regression on top of it — because the build going out to 500+ devices has never been run on a phone. This is not hypothetical. Fourteen reviewers examined this release and not one launched a native binary; every area's conclusion routes to "upload iOS" and nobody looked at what would be uploaded. Meanwhile the build carries brand-new native keyboard code (a4c572b, 182d64c, three days old, touching the exact subsystem your memory file names as the root of two previously shipped bugs), a report button that lies about success, a rate prompt on a loose timer, and the first appearance of the ratings engine on iOS ever. iOS is ~95% of your native base and the review cycle is days long, so a regression that ships is a regression that lives for a week on the only surface that generates store reviews. With 8 ratings across five storefronts and no aggregate visible to anyone, two bad reviews in the wrong storefront is a visible, durable change to what a cold visitor sees. The mirror risk, and it is real too, is that you do nothing because the list feels long. It is not long. The sign-up dead end is doing damage right now — 103 of 218 accounts have never finished a game — and the fix is already written and merged. Half a day of edits and one evening with your phone is the entire distance between "cannot ship" and "should ship". That is the brick. Two smaller risks worth naming. First, that someone applies the panel's quick wins literally: two of them are actively destructive — clearing celebrationTimeoutsRef on a screen change silently deletes your entire celebration layer, and branching quiz.js:159 desyncs the frozen Daily 7 — and I verified both against the source rather than taking the critic's word. Read the danger flags in the plan before touching either file. Second, that we keep grading ourselves on how much we found rather than on how much reached a player. The most useful number in this report is not any grade. It is 47.2%.
