# IG follow conversion: why views don't become follows, and what to put on the last slide

Researched 2026-09-24 for @shithouseryhq (IG 32,553). Every source carries a date. **E** = evidence (a platform statement, a meta-analysis or our own numbers). **S** = speculation, a plausible mechanism that nobody has measured for us.

---

## 0. TL;DR

1. **The last slide is not the main leak.** Across the account, views turn into profile visits at about 0.23% (1.10M views → 2,511 profile views, 8 days to 09-23). Visits turn into follows at about 15–20% (~430 follows in the same 8 days), which is normal (vendor benchmarks put the average near 13.5%). Per post: "Day five" got **4 follows from 5 profile visits**. Once people look at the profile, they follow. What's missing is people looking.
2. **Suspect #1 is recommendability, not the copy.** On 2026-04-30 Instagram stopped recommending accounts whose photos and carousels are mostly other people's content, and it said a *"screenshot that shows the original poster's name isn't enough"* to count as original. That describes our tweet carousels exactly. If we're flagged, most of our reach goes to people who already follow us, and **existing followers can't follow again**. The best CTA in the world can't fix that.
3. **CTAs don't separate the winners.** 3 of the 5 big carousel accounts we sweep use no follow CTA at all.
4. **Our only follow-earning feed posts** were (a) a *numbered series* post and (b) our *own original* maths card. Build CTAs and levers around those two facts.

**What this tells us:**
- A CTA isn't what makes the big accounts grow.
- Where a CTA works, it does one of two jobs: it **shows the button** (ftblmemeshub's arrow, plus social proof from the circled count) or it **is itself a meme** (nonoffsideguy).
- None of them use a random celebrity photo plus "tap follow", which is our current pattern.
- The largest accounts are visibly worried about the originality flag. That's behaviour we observed; nobody has proven the padding works.

## 3. What the 2025–26 evidence says drives follows

**Platform (E):**
- **2026-04-30, Instagram (TechCrunch, Tubefilter):** accounts that mostly repost other people's content in photos and carousels are "no longer … recommendable" in Feed and Explore. Followers still see them. Memes with "unique text, creative edits … a relatable take" count as original. A screenshot showing the original poster's name doesn't.
- **Jan 2025, Mosseri:** the top signals are watch time, likes per reach and **sends per reach**. Sends matter most for reaching non-followers.
- **2024-11, @instagramcreators:** "creators who post to Stories often see fewer unfollows." This is correlational, but it's Instagram's own data. Our ~25 unfollows a day are half the problem.
- **2025-04-08, Mosseri (Social Media Today):** Feed reaches far more people than Stories, and hashtags are no longer a main reach lever.
- **Carousels are re-served** starting from the first unseen slide (Buffer 2026 guide). The **last slide does get seen**, but only by people who swiped through, and those are mostly the engaged ones.

**Behavioural science, mapped honestly:**

| Mechanism | Evidence quality | Does it fit a feed? |
|---|---|---|
| **Door-in-the-face** | Meta-analysis (Feeley et al. 2012): works for *verbal* agreement (r=.13) but **not for behaviour** (r=.05, n.s.). Needs the same person to ask twice, face to face. | **No.** Drop it. |
| **Foot-in-the-door** | Burger 1999 review: small and conditional (needs a real first act and self-perception). | **Weak.** Best version: get a comment first, reply to it, and let repeated exposure do the rest. Not a slide. |
| **Zeigarnik / open loops** | Ghibellini & Meier 2025 meta-analysis (59 studies): **no memory boost** for unfinished tasks (d≈0.15). The **Ovsiankina resumption drive** does hold: 67% resume vs 50% baseline. | **Yes, as a series.** Promise a specific next instalment instead of leaving a cliffhanger. |
| **Peak-end rule** | Kahneman et al. 1993, well replicated: people judge an experience by its peak and its ending. | **Yes.** The last slide shouldn't be a dip. Put the best joke last. |
| **Social proof from peers** | Cialdini: strongest when it comes from similar, known others. | **Yes.** The DM recipient already knows one follower: the friend who sent it. |
| **Identity nouns** ("be one of us") | Bryan et al. 2011 found it; Gerber et al. 2016 **failed to replicate** it at scale. | **Maybe.** Club tribalism is real, but label effects are shaky. |
| **Reciprocity / value promise** | A follow is a bet on *future* posts. Clarity about what's coming is the lever (S, but basic). | **Yes.** Say what, when and how often. |

## 4. Eight final-slide concepts in our voice

Rules they all follow: photo and line are one joke, the premise is true (fact-checker pass), and no line gets reused within a week.

**A. "Day N" series card**: *Ovsiankina resumption + a specific value promise.* ⭐ Top 3
Image: an empty Premier League stadium, seats up (Commons).
Sticker: **"Day 6 of 17 without the Prem. / Day 7 is tomorrow. We'll be here."**
It's the format of our only follow-earning carousel, made explicit. After the break, the same idea becomes "Matchweek 8, the Monday post."

**B. "Someone sent you this"**: *peer social proof, aimed at the non-follower DM audience.* ⭐ Top 3
Image: two teammates, one whispering in the other's ear.
Sticker: **"If a mate sent you this, / he already follows. Catch up."**
It's written for exactly the people who can still follow: the ones reached by our 23.8 shares per 1k.

**C. "HERE WE GO"**: *show the button (ftblmemeshub's mechanism) as a transfer-announcement parody.* ⭐ Top 3
Image: a screenshot of **our real profile header**, with an arrow from Follow to Following.
Text: **"HERE WE GO! 🚨 You → Shithousery HQ. / Contract until 2030. Medical done in one tap."**
It's funny to football fans and removes all doubt about the action. Use the phrase only, no Fabrizio avatar or name.

**D. Shithousery on the viewer**: *the brand name explains the account (niche clarity).*
Image: a keeper down "injured" or a player shielding the ball at the corner flag.
Sticker: **"We'll stay down here / until you tap follow."**
The account's name is the joke, so the viewer also learns what the account is for.

**E. Absurd maths**: *our proven format with a tiny ask.*
Image: an own-card league-table crop.
Sticker: **"Mathematically, if everyone who swiped this far followed, / we'd gain more today than Spurs have points."** Recompute before every use; this one depends on the fact-checker.

**F. Best joke last**: *peak-end + reciprocity, the no-CTA competitor style done better.*
Image: the strongest slide of the set, moved to the end.
Small corner sticker: **"Saved the best for last. 5 of these a day."**

**G. "Your club's next"**: *identity + a scheduled promise. It mirrors the bio line and a posting rota.*
Image: the fixture list for the next matchweek.
Sticker: **"Arsenal got it today. / Check the fixtures. You're not safe."** Only use it if we actually run a club rota.

**H. Honest underdog**: *self-deprecating data joke; use once a fortnight at most.*
Image: a deadline-day-style board (no broadcaster branding).
Sticker: **"26 people unfollowed us yesterday. / Window's open. Replace one."**

**Retire:** achievement → "one tap" (Ancelotti, Bale, Kane, Messi, Yamal). They're all the same skeleton, the photo has no connection to the line, and the audience now reads them as a format. **Also retire** the reaction memes with no premise (Pep, Foden, Potter, Mbappé). They beg without saying why, and Alex rejected "begging."

## 5. Non-CTA levers, ranked by expected impact

1. **Recommendability and originality (biggest by far, E).**
   - First check Settings → Account Status → Recommendations.
   - In the app, check the *followers vs non-followers* split on 5 recent carousels.
   - If non-followers are under ~30%, every carousel mostly reaches people who can't follow.
   - Fix: raise the share of own originals (the maths cards earned follows). Run the existing `--takes-on-slide` A/B: Instagram's own definition counts "unique text" on a meme as original, and a bare username screenshot as not.
2. **Stories every day (E, but correlational).**
   - Repost each carousel to Story, plus 1–2 native Stories.
   - Instagram says this lowers unfollows. Cutting ~25/day to ~18 is worth as much net growth as a CTA that doubles follows.
3. **Named, numbered series + 3 pinned openers (E from our data, S on size).**
   - Run "Day N of the break", then a weekly club rota (Mon United, Tue Arsenal…, matching the YouTube series).
   - Pin the three best series posts, so a profile visit shows "this is a daily show."
4. **Collab posts with club fan pages (S, supported by the mechanism).**
   - For club-specific carousels, invite a mid-size fan account as co-author. The post then appears in their followers' feeds, which is a large dose of fresh, non-follower reach.
5. **Caption ending + first-hour replies (S).**
   - End on the series promise ("Day 7 tomorrow") or a specific debate question, never generic bait.
   - Reply to the first 10 comments and pin the funniest one. Mosseri says replies don't rank directly (2025-04-08), but they bring commenters back, which is the only foot-in-the-door that works here.

## 6. Two-week A/B test (start 09-28, after the reel-test read on 09-27)

- **Step 0 (a gate):** record the non-follower share of 5 carousels and the Account Status screenshot. If recommendations are off, fix that first. Otherwise the test is measured on people who already follow.
- **Arms (4):**
  - **Control:** current punchline CTA.
  - **A:** Day-N series.
  - **B:** Someone-sent-you-this.
  - **F:** Best joke last, no ask.
  - Rotate arms by slot, `arm = (day + slot) mod 4`, so time of day and news days spread evenly. Everything else stays fixed: 10 slides, the sweep method, the CTA always last.
- **Log** each post as `date, media_id, arm, concept` in `social/state/cta_test.tsv`.
- **Primary metric:** Σfollows ÷ Σviews × 1000 per arm. Read each post at **48h** (`insights.mjs --hours 72`) and pool per arm; don't average per-post ratios. **Secondary metrics:** profile visits/1k, follows per visit, shares/1k (a CTA mustn't cost shares). **Guardrail:** daily account follows vs the 38–63 baseline.
- **Power, stated plainly:**
  - 5–6 carousels a day × 14 days ≈ 20 per arm ≈ 120–150K views per arm. At ~0.1 follows/1k, that's ~12–15 follows per arm.
  - Only a **~2× difference** is detectable (one-sided Poisson rate test, p<0.1).
  - Decision: adopt an arm only if it beats control by ≥1.5× and passes that test. Otherwise report "no detectable difference" and choose on Alex's taste. **C (Here We Go)** and **E (maths)** are next in the queue.
